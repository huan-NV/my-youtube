Hello, the story I'm going to tell you today took place on March 20, 1945, at 7 AM on a Tuesday morning in an alley on Tan Suong Street, Shanghai, China. The daily rhythm of life gradually awakened each household, with wooden doors creaking open and closed continuously, carrying the rustic breath of urban life. That alley was Jiang Yuan Alley.

The alley was named after Zhang Zhenheng Jiang Yuan, a famous figure at the time, and was located near the intersection of Jiao Lu, Tan Cha Lu, and Huang He Lu. This alley ran east to west, less than 150 meters long, with dozens of ancient houses densely packed on both sides.

Although the alley itself wasn't worth much, it was home to hundreds of families from all walks of life, from merchants and apprentices to dancers. In the morning, the alley entrance was busier than ever, with all kinds of street food stalls, breakfast shops, and constant delivery activity. Because it was built long ago, the alley's surface was paved with uneven stones. Whenever it rained, water would pool everywhere, causing severe flooding.

Even worse, when the sewage truck passed by, residents might dump their personal waste bins onto the street with the rainwater. At such times, the alley was no different from a public toilet, heavy with an unbearable stench. As a result, Jiang Yuan Alley was viewed by outsiders as a dirty, shabby place.

At house number 80 at the end of the alley, there was a fortune-telling shop on the ground floor run by Wang Xieyang. Mr. Wang made his living as a fortune teller, but he wasn't the owner of the building - he was just a tenant.

He only used the ground floor for his business, while he had rented out the upstairs space. On that morning, when he and his wife woke up to use the bathroom, they happened to notice a red liquid stain on the stair railing. Looking up, they discovered that the liquid was dripping from the second floor.

The couple tore off a piece of white paper to wipe it, only to realize in horror that it wasn't water but blood. Both husband and wife were startled and frightened. Wang Xieyang stood frozen for a moment before rushing upstairs to knock on the door.
------->
Five minutes later, the door slowly opened. As soon as Mr. Wang stepped inside, a chill ran down his spine. In the room, blood was splattered everywhere.

Inside, Zhang Zhishi was standing there, her hands covered in blood. Her expression was blank, showing no sign of evasion, as if completely lost in her own world. Wang Xieyang had a terrible premonition.

He walked over to the most blood-stained trunk, intending to open it and check inside. At that moment, Zhang Zhishi's expression suddenly changed, and she rushed forward to stop him. But it was too late.

When the trunk was opened, a bloody head appeared before their eyes. Wang Xieyang felt dizzy, his face turned pale, and his body swayed unsteadily. At this point, Zhang Zhishi stood beside him and slowly spoke.

Her voice was hoarse, as if her throat had been scraped by something, saying that she had killed her husband. Her husband was Zhang Yunding. Zhang Yunding was 30 years old, from Anhui, 1.85 meters tall, and weighed 100kg.

With his robust build, he was nicknamed "The Porter". He had worked at a nearby pawnshop, and later opened his own store selling second-hand clothes and other used items, which was quite popular.

His personality was completely opposite to his name Yunding, which means clear and handsome. Instead, he was a rough, crude, and dissolute person. As for his wife, Zhang Zhishi, she was 29 years old that year, from Dayang, Jiangsu. Her original surname was Du, and she was an orphan. After being adopted by the Zhang family, she changed her surname to Zhang, with the full name Zhang Chunlan.

The Zhang family treated her well, never showing any contempt, and even arranged a marriage match for her. The marriage between Zhang Zhishi and Zhang Yunding was arranged by the Zhang family. At that time, Zhang Yunding saw that she had outstanding looks and was the same age, so he happily agreed. As for Zhang Zhishi, being just an adopted child living on others' charity, she basically had no right to refuse and could only passively accept.

Thus, she married reluctantly and changed her surname to her husband's, becoming Zhang Zhishi at the age of 21. In the eyes of others, although the couple couldn't be said to have deep love and affection, it wasn't to the extent that would lead to killing one's spouse. About an hour before the discovery of the incident, at 6 AM, Wang Xieyang, in a half-awake state, had vaguely heard strange noises and screams from upstairs.

At that time, he thought it was just a domestic matter and reflexively went upstairs quietly, standing outside the door to ask what was happening. Zhang Zhishi didn't open the door but vaguely replied through the gap that Zhang Yunding was talking in his sleep. Although Mr. Wang felt it was strange, he didn't feel it appropriate to ask more questions, so he went back downstairs to continue sleeping.

Unexpectedly, such a horrifying incident occurred. Still in shock, Mr. Wang quickly instructed his wife to watch Zhang Zhishi carefully. Then he rushed to the nearest police station.

He was so tense because he feared being implicated. In Shanghai at that time, surrounding residential areas would monitor and supervise each other to control law violations. If violations were discovered, they had to be reported immediately, or one could be held jointly responsible.
----->
The police received the report and quickly arrived at the scene, cordoning off both ends of Jiang Yuan Alley and beginning their investigation. The room where the Zhang couple lived was dark and filled with an eerie atmosphere. There was blood on the bed, on the floor, and even in the water bucket. These were clearly traces of this horrifying crime.

Zhang Yunding's body had been dismembered into 16 pieces and placed in a large wooden trunk. The victim's throat, arms, fingers, and abdomen all had multiple wounds. When Zhang Zhishi was escorted downstairs, she wore only thin clothes, speaking in a timid, almost soulless voice.

She said to the police: "Please, help me, sir." At first, the police thought she was begging for leniency for her crime. But when they saw her timid demeanor, they understood.

The police got her a coat to wear. With that, she could maintain some dignity. During the escort, crowds of nearby residents gathered to watch.

One of the underage residents at that time, decades later when interviewed, still clearly remembered that scene. With intense curiosity, the boy had struggled through the crowd to get to the front to see for himself. But was surprised to see the trunk containing the corpse that hadn't been moved yet.

That sight caused a great shock to the boy's innocent mind, making him exclaim. How could Zhang Zhishi be so cruel, to treat her husband so brutally? At the police station, Zhang Zhishi remained calm, frankly admitting to killing her husband. When asked about the reason, she said: "My husband treated me too violently, too cruelly, I hated him so I had to kill him."

Zhang Zhishi also firmly stated that the entire crime was committed by her alone, with no one else involved. However, the police didn't believe her words, because the victim had a large build. Meanwhile, Zhang Zhishi was small, only 1.50 meters tall and weighing just 40kg.

The large physical disparity made the crime much more difficult to commit. With this thought, an idea occurred to the police. They suspected that Zhang Zhishi might have a lover who had helped her commit the crime.

In another development, the crime scene had been surrounded by reporters who had heard the news, packed so tightly that not even water could get through. Although the house had been cordoned off, they still managed to extract information from neighbors, trying to understand the details of the case. Their suspicions matched with the police's inclination toward speculation about her and a lover committing the crime.

To attract attention, they exaggerated and sensationalized Zhang Zhishi's improper behavior in their articles, criticizing her for not maintaining her duties as a wife, painting her as an evil woman. To add drama, they even fabricated that the person who reported the crime, Wang Xieyang, was blind, and that he discovered the blood because it dripped directly onto his face. For a time, the case became a hot topic of discussion.

People who read the newspapers all criticized Zhang Zhishi's behavior. Rumors spread from unknown sources that Zhang Zhishi would be publicly paraded to shame her, and even the parade route was mapped out in a seemingly credible way. This rumor spread widely and caused congestion on related roads in the city, with crowds gathering densely, many people craning their necks waiting to see Zhang Zhishi's face.

It wasn't until the police officially denied it that the rumors subsided. But was the truth really as the media and police were thinking? From a modern perspective, perhaps people can easily spot the inconsistencies in it. However, in an era when information was still closed off like that time, even if Zhang Zhishi had a thousand words to say, she couldn't defend herself.

In the first year after marriage, her husband Zhang Yunding hadn't fully revealed his true nature. At that time, he worked carefully and considerately, and his words also had some gentleness. This made Zhang Zhishi, who had endured loneliness and hardship since childhood, feel the warmth and support she had long been without.

But not long after, under the influence of bad friends, Zhang Yunding became involved in gambling, often not returning home all night. Along with the bankruptcy of his secondhand store, he became more violent. All household income was tightly controlled by him.

He frequently sought entertainment outside, living a dissipated life. The number of times he brought money home in a month could be counted on one hand. Attacking his wife was a regular occurrence for him, even in public places without any restraint.

What made Zhang Zhishi even more angry was her husband's promiscuous lifestyle. Zhang Yunding even had an affair with a prostitute to the point where the woman became pregnant. When the pregnant woman was evicted by her landlord, she desperately came to Zhang Yunding for support.

She was 8 months pregnant and about to give birth, but Zhang Yunding brazenly demanded that Zhang Zhishi handle this marriage situation for him. Reluctantly, Zhang Zhishi took this woman to the hospital and stayed by her side to care for her when she gave birth. Afterwards, she earnestly helped find a good new family for the woman, which temporarily resolved this scandal.

She had cried, had pleaded, but her husband remained indifferent. Bound by traditional concepts, Zhang Zhishi didn't dare and had never thought about divorce. To survive, she could only earn money herself.

She had tried to find work outside, even accepting a neighbor's recommendation to work at a nearby tobacco factory. Each day, she went out to work early and returned home when it was dark. Although life was hard, at least she could eat her fill.
-------->
However, in that era, the traditional concept that men handled outside affairs while women managed the household was still deeply rooted, and women working outside was often seen as a symbol of poverty. Therefore, when Zhang Yunding found out, he felt very embarrassed and flew into a rage at her. In his anger, he maliciously speculated that his wife must have a lover.

This thought made him adamantly refuse to let his wife go out, doing whatever it took to prevent Zhang Zhishi from working. At the same time, he still insisted on controlling all household income. Even worse, after getting drunk one time, he attacked Zhang Zhishi so severely that she couldn't get out of bed for several days.

With no choice, Zhang Zhishi had to quit her job and sell or pawn valuable items from the house to maintain daily expenses. She went everywhere to borrow from friends and neighbors, but after borrowing so many times, no one would lend to her anymore. Finally, she could only survive by running tabs at the secondhand store, steamed bun shop, or even breakfast stalls.

Throughout the secondhand stores, steamed bun shops, and breakfast stalls, everywhere had records of her debts. The prolonged physical abuse and mental pressure caused Zhang Zhishi's psychological state to deteriorate increasingly, to the point where she had thoughts of doing something desperate several times. One day in October 1944, after failing to borrow money from a secondhand store, she stole a bottle of disinfectant from the shop, took it home, and decided to drink it.

Fortunately, neighbors quickly noticed something was wrong and rushed her to the hospital in time. Thanks to that, she survived. Witnessing Zhang Zhishi in such a miserable state, none of the neighbors could help but feel sympathy.

They had once pledged to pool money, hoping to help Zhang Zhishi open a small shop, but unexpectedly, all that well-intentioned money ended up in Zhang Yunding's pocket. Unable to find a way out, Zhang Zhishi had to think of other solutions. She had considered running a stationery stand in front of Jiang Yuan Elementary School.

She also thought about how there were many small stalls gathered at the entrance of Jiang Yuan Alley, and she was good at making potato-fried pork chops. After some consideration, she finally decided to open a stall selling potato-fried pork chops. This thought made her feel more hopeful than ever.

Happily pawning her red jacket and using the money to buy a vegetable knife in preparation for chopping meat later. Therefore, at 3 AM on March 20, Zhang Yunding returned home drunk. Seeing that he seemed to be in a good mood, Zhang Zhishi carefully explained all her intentions.

Unexpectedly, after hearing this, Zhang Yunding thought it was nonsense, not only cursed her terribly but also loudly threatened that even if Zhang Zhishi really earned money, he would take all that money to gamble and spend wastefully. After saying this, he immediately started snoring and went back to sleep. Seeing all paths to making a living blocked, the anger in Zhang Zhishi's heart could no longer be contained.

Just then, a voice rang out in her head, telling her to kill, saying that killing would lead to liberation. So, after three hours of intense internal struggle, at 6 AM, she finally took the newly bought vegetable knife and struck hard at the sleeping Zhang Yunding. After killing her husband, Zhang Zhishi didn't know how to handle the body, so she learned from plot points in plays, dismembering the body into 16 pieces and putting them in a wooden trunk to destroy the evidence.

That wooden trunk was originally used to store the couple's clothes, but because Zhang Yunding spent money wastefully, the clothes in the house had either been sold or pawned. This once-full trunk was now empty, but unexpectedly, blood dripped down to the floor below, causing the crime to be discovered. Although Zhang Zhishi detailed the tragedies she had endured when questioned by police, she still didn't receive their trust.

Therefore, in another interrogation, she changed her testimony, admitting that someone had helped her commit the crime. This person was named Xia Xianhui, nicknamed Xia Dama Zi, her neighbor. Every day, Xia Xianhui often helped Zhang Zhishi.

Seeing her pitiful situation, he had helped her with money many times. Gradually, they became close, even crossing the boundaries of neighborly relations. Zhang Zhishi vaguely said that perhaps it was to repay the kindness, or perhaps feelings had developed.

She and Xia Xianhui had inappropriate relations several times. The police immediately investigated and found that the day after the incident, Xia Xianhui had left Shanghai and gone elsewhere, showing suspicious signs related to the case. Therefore, the police tried every means to find him.

Xia Xianhui admitted to having an improper relationship with Zhang Zhishi but firmly denied involvement in the murder. He frankly admitted that he had no real feelings for Zhang Zhishi, it was just circumstantial fun. Before the police could investigate clearly, Zhang Zhishi changed her testimony again, naming another person called Xia Baoyu.

This person, nicknamed Xiao Ling Ba, was a friend of Zhang Yunding. Zhang Zhishi testified that two days before the incident, she had discussed the murder plan with Xia Baoyu and had prepared many things for it. On the day of the murder, taking advantage of her husband's deep sleep, she had opened the door for Xia Baoyu and given him a vegetable knife.

Then watched as Xia Baoyu struck her husband's neck. After her husband died, she added another knife wound to his neck. This testimony greatly confused the police, so they went to find Xia Baoyu.

But Xia Baoyu was very frightened, couldn't defend himself, and strongly denied participating in the crime. The case took another turn a week later. Zhang Zhishi revised her testimony, saying that both people she had previously mentioned were actually not involved in this case.

She revealed that she had implicated Xia Xianhui entirely due to being tortured and coerced by the police for a long time, causing her mind to become unclear, leading to a momentary outburst. As for blaming Xia Baoyu, it was because she resented him. Previously, Xia Baoyu had repeatedly encouraged her husband to gamble.

In her heart, her husband had been corrupted by Xia Baoyu. After police investigation, both these people had alibis at the time of the incident, so the suspicions were eliminated. More than a month after the incident occurred, in May 1945, the case officially went to trial.

Zhang Zhishi was charged with murder. At the trial, she wore a gray jacket, was thin and haggard, but her clothes were still neat. She maintained a calm expression, recounting the details of her crime.

For each question from the judge, she gave systematic answers. The court believed that Zhang Zhishi had not fled after committing the crime. Her statements in court were logically consistent.

This showed she was completely lucid when committing the crime and fully criminally responsible. Finally, the court sentenced Zhang Zhishi to death, with the judge giving 16 characters as reason: failed self-defense, love cannot forgive, dismembered body, extremely cruel. The Wang couple received a reward for reporting the crime in time.

Before the execution, Zhang Zhishi was detained at the Tieh Lan Prison in Shanghai. Regarding this result, public opinion was mainly divided into two camps. One group firmly held to the concept that killing must be paid with life, fully supporting the verdict.

They viewed Zhang Zhishi as a modern money-earning woman and thought she deserved this fate. The other side expressed sympathy for Zhang Zhishi, believing she was a victim of domestic violence. The killing of her husband was simply a desperate resistance when driven into a corner.

They felt that the death penalty for her was too harsh. Among those who supported this view, there were many writers and intellectuals who later wrote articles in magazines and newspapers to speak up for Zhang Zhishi.

Among them was Su Cheng, one of the four famous female talents in the literary world at that time. In her article defending the husband killer, she left sharp words. Only those who endure mistreatment day after day develop cruelty.

If so, that person who shed blood is a hero. If a person sheds blood, must they be considered a criminal? Su Cheng even directly named the reason why Zhang Zhishi killed her husband. She had also done something foolish but couldn't die.

So she only had the option to kill. After the article was published, Su Cheng immediately became the focus of public opinion. Some people even took advantage of this to attack her.

Saying she was also improper like Zhang Zhishi, which is why she defended her. However, Su Cheng persisted in her views. Later, she published several more articles to clarify her position.

At her wit's end, she used sharp words in her article "Zhang Zhishi and Fan Jiexian" to counter that men are all hateful. So why can't hateful men be killed? The feeling of empathy grew like a snowball, attracting more people to care about the suffering that Zhang Zhishi had endured. Under strong public pressure, the execution of the death penalty was postponed time and again, and some lawyers volunteered to actively propose free protection for Zhang Zhishi, seeking a glimmer of hope for her survival.

Hearing understanding voices from the public, Zhang Zhishi was deeply moved and changed her passive attitude of waiting for death, beginning to appeal. She performed well in prison, and after being assigned to an embroidery group, thanks to her innate skillfulness, her embroidery works were not only high quality but also produced quickly, becoming an exemplary model. And the appearance of a key figure caused her death sentence to be further delayed.

This person was an elderly nun from a Christian church who testified for Zhang Zhishi at the appeal hearing. She said that Zhang Zhishi was pregnant and the child was innocent, even if the death penalty had to be carried out, it should wait until after the birth. In fact, the so-called pregnancy was completely untrue.

However, under the medical conditions at that time, it was impossible to accurately verify the authenticity of the pregnancy. Therefore, the court could only rely on the testimony and temporarily postpone the execution of the death penalty. Unexpectedly, before Zhang Zhishi could give birth in August 1945, Japan announced its unconditional surrender, along with the dissolution and reconstruction of the original judicial organs, previous judgments were also considered invalid.

In August 1946, a year and a half after the incident occurred, the Shanghai High Court began to retry this case. Zhang Zhishi wore a blue shirt looking particularly haggard, her shoulder-length hair highlighting the gloom between her eyebrows. She revealed more details about the case, saying that on the day of the murder, she had originally intended to go to the bathroom, accidentally saw a knife in the drawer.

At that moment, she felt as if possessed by a demon, picked up the knife and just did it. That knife was originally bought to open a pork chop and potato fries stall, unexpectedly becoming a weapon that took a life. She emphasized that the case was not a premeditated action, but the result of a moment of impulse.

Her account was clearer than in the previous trial, and statements favorable to herself also increased significantly. This was perhaps due to the guidance of lawyers. When asked whether Zhang Yunding made any sound at the time of the murder.

Zhang Zhishi thought for a long time before giving a negative answer. She declared that she had been unconscious at the time, not even realizing that her finger had been accidentally injured. The defense lawyer pleaded for Zhang Zhishi's mercy, focusing on describing her tragic circumstances.

Even more surprisingly, the lawyer revealed a shocking detail, disclosing that Zhang Yunding was suspected of having connections with the Japanese side. Therefore, Zhang Zhishi's killing of him could perhaps be considered meritorious service. As soon as these words were spoken, the entire courtroom burst into laughter.

As for whether this was true or not, there is no longer any basis for verification. But these words certainly demonstrated the lawyer's efforts to help Zhang Zhishi get a reduced sentence. After a month of trial in September, the court declared the defendant Zhang Zhishi guilty and sentenced her to death.

Zhang Zhishi did not contest the verdict and appealed. Before the trial, although in a final situation, Zhang Zhishi still received attention from all walks of society. Occasionally reporters would inquire about news about her in prison and publish it in newspapers.

These news items were mostly trivial details but sparked public curiosity. For example, she had gained weight, was in good health, got along well with other prisoners, and behaved properly in prison. All levels of society continuously spoke up for her. The vivid writing style of writers and the eloquence of lawyers together created a solid protective network for her.

But their efforts were not in vain. Nearly 3 years after the incident occurred, Zhang Zhishi's sentence was reduced to 15 years in prison. After learning the result, Zhang Zhishi couldn't contain her joy, as if coming back to life.

She continuously thanked all those who had helped her. In 1949, Zhang Zhishi was transferred to the Shanghai Farm in Tai Feng, Jiangsu to serve her sentence. There, she had the opportunity to apply for bail assistance.

To regain her freedom, she had written to Wang Xieyang, earnestly asking him to bail her out. However, after the letter was sent with no reply, she later learned that Wang Xieyang didn't refuse to help but didn't dare agree, so she had to give up. After completing her prison term, Zhang Zhishi chose to settle in Tai Feng.

At that time, she was completely alone, with no relatives, no friends, and no fixed residence. Through the arrangements of the authorities, she went to work at a Turkish company. It was in this job that she met her husband who was two years younger than her, Yan Shaohua.

He knew about her past but didn't mind. They married in 1959 and spent the rest of their lives together. Zhang Zhishi especially cherished this hard-won marriage.

When she married, she was 46 years old. Happiness came late in life, but it finally arrived. This case, due to lack of photographic documentation, was largely passed down in text form, so many details in it have many rumors.

Among them, there were even rumors spreading that Zhang Zhishi had been hanged in prison as a warning, causing the public to mistakenly believe she had died long ago. To clarify the truth, reporter Ci Bing from the Shanghai Bund newspaper conducted an investigative interview in 1990. He followed the guidance of local residents to a residential house in Da Fang and nervously knocked on the door, waiting for a long time with no response.

Just as he was about to look through the window, a slightly hoarse voice startled him. "Who are you looking for?" He turned around to discover the speaker was a small but agile elderly woman. He immediately recognized that it was Zhang Zhishi, or more accurately, Zhou Huizhen.

After being released from prison, she had changed her name. That day, she still retained the rustic appearance from the past. Time had carved delicate wrinkles on her face.

Although she was 74 years old that year, her eyes still showed a kind of resilience. Sometimes they even showed a somewhat stern determination. Ci Bing guessed that this was probably closely related to the half-life of suffering she had endured.

Zhou Huizhen opened the door for Ci Bing and agreed to the interview. In the house, she was simple. She mentioned that besides this house, all the furniture in the house was bought by herself.

Speaking of this, she smiled, quite happily. Now, she had over 100 yuan in monthly pension. At that time, this amount was enough for her to live a comfortable life without worrying about food and clothing.

Before the murder occurred, Zhou Huizhen had no children. Going to prison further deprived her of the chance to become a mother. Although she had no biological children, she had a deep love for children.

She really liked her work at the Turkish company and worked there until retirement. In her house, there were pictures and images of children everywhere. During her days of freedom, whenever she went somewhere, she would always adopt a few children as goddaughters and godsons to fill the deep void in her heart.

While talking with Ci Bing, Zhou Huizhen held a small girl, playing with the child in her arms while humming cheerful nursery rhymes. Every wrinkle on her face seemed to be soothed by happiness. Looking at the innocent and peaceful old woman before him, Ci Bing couldn't help but feel emotional.

Who could connect this kind and benevolent old lady with the woman once labeled as an evil wife who killed her husband years ago. Zhou Huizhen's husband had passed away from illness a few years ago. When mentioning her husband, her face showed gentleness, saying that what she cherished most was the wedding photo of her and her husband, placed under the glass on the bedside table.

However, when once again mentioning the 1945 case, she fell into a long silence. Clearly, Zhou Huizhen didn't want to recall that painful and harsh period. She just quietly said, "I want to live to old age, treat myself well."

"I killed because it was a final battle for survival. No one protected me, I could only save myself." Beyond that, she offered no further explanation.

After the interview, reporter Ci Bing hurried to the scene of the crime. After the incident occurred, that house had no occupants. Later, a seemingly braver private detective had moved in. There, Ci Bing met an elderly woman living on the third floor, surnamed Zhang, so called Mrs. Zhang.

Mrs. Zhang was very pleased to see a reporter visit and actively cooperated with the interview. She revealed a surprising detail. The first witness of the case was actually not Wang Xieyang, but herself.

Early morning on the day of the incident, when she went downstairs to get water, she accidentally touched blood on the stair railing, felt something was wrong, and had told this to Wang Xieyang and showed him. After that came the story of Wang Xieyang discovering and reporting to the police. After finishing the interview, when Ci Bing was returning along the original path, he met some local residents.

When mentioning this matter, the usually calm residents widened their eyes, filled with surprise. It turned out that in the decades after her release from prison, Zhou Huizhen had never returned to Shanghai, nor had she ever intended to learn about the changes in Jiang Yuan Alley. The residents, not having seen her for many years, were curious about her current situation.

So Ci Bing took out recent photos of Zhou Huizhen to show them, eliciting a series of sighs. With time, the area around Tan Suong Road and Jiang Yuan Alley had long been redeveloped. Now, it is a residential area with high-rise buildings, the appearance of the narrow alley from the past no longer exists, only historical traces remain.

This old murder case in Shanghai, its influence has not faded with time. Taiwanese writer Li Gang took this case as a prototype to create the novel "The Husband's Corpse". This novel not only won awards but was also translated into many languages, gaining international attention.

Later, "The Husband's Corpse" was adapted into both a movie and TV series of the same name. In 2020, the film "The Mystery of Jiang Yuan Road" adapted from this case will be released, bringing this historical case back into the public eye once again. One of the filming locations for this movie, Corpse Road, had become a hot check-in spot, crowded with people taking photos, many coming to take commemorative photos.

Zhou Huizhen had broken down her desperation into pieces but could not escape the bonds of feudal etiquette. Her story exposed the tragedy and struggle of women of the old times under a patriarchal society and the constraints of traditional concepts. Fortunately, social progress finally shouldered a heavy blow for her.

Her story, though decades have passed, still echoes like a whisper from the past reminding us of the true meaning of justice and compassion, and that sometimes an individual's survival is also a mirror reflecting a gradually awakening society. And regardless, in our current era, taking someone's life as she did is completely wrong. Today's video ends here, and I'll see you in the next videos.
